# EK FastPDF benchmarks (measured)

**Important limits.** Measurements were taken on CI/cloud machines, *not* on an Intel i7-12700 + NVIDIA T1000:
* **Windows**: GitHub Actions `windows-2022` VM – AMD EPYC 9V74, **2 cores / 4 threads**, 16 GB RAM, **no GPU**
  (Microsoft Basic Render Driver; WPF composition runs on WARP, i.e. on the CPU).
* **Linux**: development container, 4 vCPU, 15 GB RAM (engine only – the WPF viewer cannot run there).

The i7-12700 has 4–6× the parallel CPU throughput of these VMs and a real GPU, so absolute times on the target PC
will be lower. Worker count and cache size are chosen at run time from the detected hardware (7 workers on an
i7-12700), not from these measurements. **No comparison with Foxit was made** – see `WINDOWS-TEST-CHECKLIST.md` §5.
Reproduce: `EKFastPDF.Bench engine <folder>` and `EKFastPDF.exe --benchmark <folder>`.

## 1. Engine: v1.1 render path vs v1.2 worker pool
Time until **every tile covering a 2560×1440 viewport** is rendered (ms; "first" = first tile). v1.1 = the original
algorithm (global lock, page re-parsed per 768-px tile, sequential), reproduced in `LegacyEngine.cs` with the same PDFium.

### Windows CI VM (2 cores / 4 threads), 512-px tiles
| Document | View | v1.1 | v1.2, 1 worker | v1.2, 2 workers | v1.2, 4 workers |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| cad-dense | fit (cold page) | 1 439 | WIN_DENSE_FIT |
| cad-dense | zoom 400 % | 2 750 | WIN_DENSE_Z400 |
| cad-dense | pan at 400 % | 2 256 | WIN_DENSE_PAN |
| cad-dense | zoom 1600 % | 1 364 | WIN_DENSE_Z1600 |

### Linux container (4 vCPU), 512-px tiles
| Document | View | v1.1 | 1 worker | 2 workers | 3 workers | 4 workers | speed-up (4w vs v1.1) |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| cad-dense | fit (cold page) | 1 712 | 630 | 582 | 453 | 512 | 3.3× |
| cad-dense | zoom 400 % | 3 294 | 257 | 130 | 99 | 92 | **36×** |
| cad-dense | pan at 400 % | 2 137 | 243 | 128 | 99 | 95 | 22× |
| cad-dense | zoom 1600 % | 1 612 | 110 | 61 | 46 | 46 | 35× |
| cad-extreme | fit (cold page) | 6 208 | 2 390 | 1 980 | 2 318 | 1 744 | 3.6× |
| cad-extreme | zoom 400 % | 11 266 | 947 | 458 | 352 | 228 | **49×** |
| cad-extreme | pan at 400 % | 10 434 | 768 | 408 | 330 | 235 | 44× |
| cad-extreme | zoom 1600 % | 6 438 | 390 | 241 | 161 | 146 | 44× |
| image-heavy | zoom 400 % | 832 | 104 | 53 | 37 | 33 | 25× |
| many-pages (p. 301) | zoom 400 % | 105 | 105 | 56 | 36 | 35 | 3× |

Where the gain comes from: (1) parsing each page once per worker instead of once per tile – 10–15× on vector-dense
sheets even with a single worker; (2) parallel workers – 2.6–4.2× more on 4 vCPU (the "fit (cold page)" case is
dominated by the one-time page parse, which every worker performs once). Cold-page parse of the extreme sheet takes
~1.5 s on this VM; it happens once per page and worker, while the overview keeps the screen filled.

**Tile size** (4 workers, Linux): 256 px is slower on dense sheets (per-tile object culling overhead: 131 vs 92 ms
at 400 %), 1024 px delays the first tile and parallelises worse at "fit" (2 275 vs 1 744 ms extreme). **512 px is the
default.**

**Engine choice** (whole sheet at 96 dpi = 4032×2880 px, open + parse + render, one thread, Linux):
PDFium **1.1 s** vs MuPDF 1.23 `mutool draw` **2.9 s** (dense); **4.4 s vs 7.7 s** (extreme).

## 3. What to expect on the i7-12700 / T1000
Not measured. Expected from the scaling above: 7 workers on 8 P-cores ≈ 3–4× the 2-worker CI numbers for
rendering-bound steps (zoom/pan sharpness); frame pacing limited by the monitor refresh with GPU composition.
